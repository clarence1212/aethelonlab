A Private Childhood: Keeping a Child's Record in the Family's Hands

A companion that stays for eighteen years will hold the most personal record a child ever has. A promise is not enough to protect it.

Aethelon Research · Note 09 · Q4 2026 · 4 min read

Think of what a family photograph album holds, and then imagine one that also remembered every question a child asked, every idea she struggled with, and the day each one finally made sense. That is the record a lifelong learning companion would keep. It would be precious, and in the wrong hands it would be dangerous.

So the honest starting point is that a promise is not enough. Privacy has to be a property of how the thing is built.

1. Where the law has drawn a line

The European Union's AI Act is the clearest signal so far. It prohibits AI systems that infer a person's emotions in workplaces and in educational institutions, with exceptions for medical and safety purposes. The reasoning is about power: a pupil cannot easily refuse.

A family home is not a school, so that rule does not directly cover a system a family chooses for itself. But the principle behind it is sound anywhere. A child's inner life should not become a product. This is our reading as builders and is not legal advice.

2. Four commitments

First, the companion notices only what a good teacher would. It needs to know whether a child is with it or has drifted to the window. It uses that in the moment to change the lesson, and does not keep it as footage.

Second, what happens in the room stays in the room. Sound and pictures are handled inside the home. What goes into the long-term record is a conclusion, such as an idea mastered or a misunderstanding noticed.

Third, the family holds the keys. The aim is a record that we as a company cannot read, and that the family can erase.

Fourth, the record has an owner in waiting. Picture an eighteenth birthday on which a young woman is handed the story of how her own mind grew, to keep, to share or to close for good. From that day it is hers.

3. An invitation to doubt us

None of this is proven until it is built and examined by people who do not work for us. We would rather a founding family ask the hardest questions now than discover the answers later.
